# Owl Roofing · Step 2 · Short guidelines per direction

**Fixed for all three (identity locked, not an option):** the Owl logo and name unchanged; ink `#17111D`, amber `#EAA221`,
cream `#F1EFE6`; Owl's own crew video and photos lead every section; facts only from the brief's lock. The owner may take one
direction whole or mix parts ("the roof from 1, the chapter rail from 2").

Boards: `OWL-DIRECTION-1-OPEN-THE-ROOF.jpg`, `OWL-DIRECTION-2-THE-CREWS-OWN.jpg`, `OWL-DIRECTION-3-FIELD-MANUAL.jpg`.

## 1 · Open the Roof (explorable depth)
- **Idea:** the roof is the hero object. The crew film sets the scene, then the house appears as a lit 3D roof built from
  Owl's own footage (shingle, decking and chimney brick are textures cut from the crew film), and the pointer lifts its
  courses to show what is underneath. Mechanism sources: Igloo (an object you open), Lusion (a travelling card carries
  you between scenes).
- **Colours as used:** ink `#17111D` is the ground (about 75%); cream `#F1EFE6` for type (about 20%); amber `#EAA221` is
  light: seam glow, rails, calls to action. Never an amber background field.
- **Fonts:** Montserrat 800/700 for headlines; Inter 400/600 for reading text (18 to 20 px).
- **Motif system:** shingle course field (half-tab offset), the lift (a tab rises over amber seam light), layer stack
  (shingle, underlayment, decking, rafter), nail line as section divider, ridge cap and drip edge as page start and end,
  the travelling video card. Motifs are filled with Owl footage textures, not bare outlines.
- **Cursor companion:** a flat bar. Resting: follows with a soft lag. Over the roof: slides under a course and prises it
  up. Over a call to action: becomes a tab set square, ready to nail. Off on touch screens.
- **Background:** at rest the courses lie closed; on hover the nearest courses lift and the decking glows amber, then close
  behind the pointer.
- **Works best for:** the depth and play the owner asked for; the Wise Check and the one game-like section sit naturally
  inside it.
- **Risks:** the 3D roof must reach photographic quality in Step 3 or it reads as a game asset; WebGL adds weight to the
  single HTML file; needs a still-image fallback for reduced motion and weak phones; a dark ground needs care with
  contrast.
- **Differs from Elbaz:** Elbaz shows finished rooms in edited video tiles on a light editorial page; Owl hands you the roof
  itself, a lit object on a dark stage that you open course by course.

## 2 · The Crew's Own (crew-fronted warmth)
- **Idea:** a documentary of the job told by the people who do it. Owl's footage runs full bleed in four chapters
  (01 The crew, 02 The work, 03 The cleanup, 04 The guarantee) with a progress rail; cream interludes read like a crew's
  field notebook. Mechanism sources: Cowboy (crew-fronted chapters, stepped reveals), Scout (pinned film scenes).
- **Colours as used:** cream `#F1EFE6` is the page (about 55%); ink `#17111D` for type and film shade (about 35%); amber
  `#EAA221` for chalk lines, the progress rail and calls to action.
- **Fonts:** Poppins 700/600 for headlines; Lato 400/700 for reading text (19 to 21 px).
- **Motif system:** a chalk line snapped on decking (section dividers), a tape blade (the chapter progress rail), the
  magnet sweep (cleanup), wrap netting (the Protect Your Nest field), a crew tally for counts from the facts lock, and a
  stepped reveal that opens photos on a staircase mask.
- **Cursor companion:** a chalk reel. Resting: its hook marks the point. Over a photo: hooks the frame and snaps a line;
  amber dust settles. Over the cleanup chapter: becomes the magnet and nails jump to it. Off on touch screens.
- **Background:** at rest faint layout lines on cream; on hover one fresh amber line snaps through the pointer and fades
  in two seconds. Never a trail.
- **Works best for:** trust through people; the warmest and the easiest to read.
- **Risks:** closest of the three to other video-led contractor sites (including Elbaz's video-led layout), so the chalk
  and chapter system must stay strong; least explorable depth; several frames carry motion blur, so the best frames must be
  chosen per chapter.
- **Differs from Elbaz:** Elbaz curates a showcase of finished spaces; Owl lets its own crew tell the job, from bare
  rafters to the last magnet sweep, in their own footage on a warm cream page.

## 3 · Field Manual (engineering doctrine)
- **Idea:** Owl as a firm that works to a written standard. Hard-cut ink blocks, numbered doctrine, a hard-number strip
  from the facts lock, and split covers that set the bare structure beside the finished roof on an amber seam. Mechanism
  sources: Longbow (numbered doctrine, hard cuts), MERSI (split covers), AirPods (one image and one number per pinned beat).
- **Colours as used:** cream `#F1EFE6` drafting field and ink `#17111D` hard blocks (about 45% each); amber `#EAA221` for
  seams, numbers and calls to action.
- **Fonts:** Inter 800 for headlines; Roboto 400/500/700/900 for reading text, numbers and labels.
- **Motif system:** pitch triangle (rise over run), section detail with Owl footage fills, dimension lines that carry only
  locked numbers, doctrine numerals in hard squares, the split cover on one seam, a 40 px measured grid as the page field.
- **Cursor companion:** a speed square. Resting: rides the pointer, heel down. Over a roof line: pivots to the slope and
  reads it. Over a call to action: drops a plumb line. Off on touch screens.
- **Background:** at rest the measured grid; on hover the square's crosshair throws dimension lines to the nearest block.
  No number is drawn unless it comes from the facts lock.
- **Works best for:** trust through proof; the clearest split from Elbaz's "finished in record time" message.
- **Risks:** can feel cold; less warmth and less play than the owner's depth law asks for; the hard-number strip is limited
  to the few locked numbers (10-year, 5.0 from 90+, 29 towns, 2025).
- **Differs from Elbaz:** Elbaz sells speed ("finished in record time") in soft editorial layouts; Owl publishes its doctrine
  and its numbers in hard-cut blocks: built to a standard, not a deadline.
