What good roofing involves

A roof has to work
as a whole.

The main covering is only part of the job. Valleys, edges, flashings, ridges and junctions all need to work together to keep water out and move it safely away from the building.

Covering
Tiles or slates laid and fixed correctly
Junctions
Leadwork and weathering where roof surfaces meet
Finishing
Ridges, edges and details checked as part of the whole roof

Repair or replacement?

Not every roof problem needs the same answer.

Repair
A local repair may be enough when the problem is isolated and the surrounding roof is still in reasonable condition.
Maintain
Some wear can be monitored or maintained when wider work is not yet justified.
Investigate
If the visible signs do not explain the problem, further inspection may be needed before deciding what to do.
Replace
Replacement may make more sense when condition, extent of wear or repeated defects make local repairs poor value.

The right recommendation should follow the condition of the roof, not the size of the job.

A simple repair example

When a repair is enough.

If an inspection finds an isolated fault and the surrounding roof is still serviceable, the proposed work can stay focused on the affected area rather than widening the job unnecessarily.

The problem
Water is getting in around a roof junction.
What was found
The defect is local; the surrounding covering is still serviceable.
Recommendation
Repair the junction rather than renew the whole roof.
Proposed work
Repair the junction and replace only the affected covering.
Final check
The repaired area is checked against the agreed work before handover.

Practical questions

“What happens if more work is found?”

If something new is uncovered, it should be explained before any extra work goes ahead. You need to understand what was found, why it changes the agreed work and any effect on cost or timing before deciding how to proceed.
