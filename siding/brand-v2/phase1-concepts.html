AUSTIN-PROMPT-V2 · Phase 1 · territory T1 The Profile Cut

Austin Pro Siding: three logo concepts

All three are drawn from one physical fact: cut a lap-sided wall at the corner and each board shows as a wedge, thin at its top, thick at its butt, lapped over the one below. Each concept uses a different method (S0609). Every mark is monochrome first and shown in black, white and steel-9, the one accent.

The territory was picked against criteria fixed before comparison (TERRITORY-SELECTION.md): T1 40/50, T3 34/50, T2 25/50. Sizes: the full mark at 48 px and above; each concept's 16 px variant at 32 px and below.

Observed live on 2026-11-02. The glyph is built from flat diamond planes shown on a visible hairline grid. How the planes overlap is shown with no shading at all: the build of the glyph explains the product (a box from folded planes). Annex v3: settled, CSSDA WOTY 2025.
Adapted
The mechanism: a glyph assembled from flat planes, whose overlap is drawn with a plain gap instead of shading.
Original
The planes are tapered boards taken from a real lap-siding section; the glyph is a letter (A); the overlap is a lap joint at the ridge; the crossbar is a course tucked behind.
Distance check (4 differences)
  1. A letter vs a box.
  2. Two tapered wedges and a bar vs four equal diamonds.
  3. Steel vs Dropbox blue.
  4. Overlap shown by a lap gap where the boards cross vs planes that only meet edge to edge.

The glyph sits in a rounded-square container, and the container is carried white on a full-bleed brand-colour field. The logo SVG is a rounded square (rx about 7.8 on 150) plus one path. The container is the brand's unit: colour as content.
Adapted
The mechanism: a container mark that is itself the colour unit, carried on a field of the brand colour.
Original
There is no glyph inside. The content is cut out of the container: the board sections, in negative space. The cuts break the container's own edge.
Distance check (5 differences)
  1. No symbol inside the tile; the cuts are the content.
  2. A broken, notched edge vs an intact rounded square.
  3. Steel vs green.
  4. Radius 5 on 56 (9%) and a 56:54 tile vs their 5% radius on a square.
  5. No currency meaning.

Recommendation: C1 The Profile House

The three concepts are argued against the two tests the brief names: ownability (S0609, question 5: why does the whole thing feel ownable?) and identifiability (S0659: could a stranger name the brand from a screenshot?).

OwnabilityIdentifiabilityHaviv
C1 The Profile HouseMedium on its own (it is still a house outline). High as a system: the same line runs on as the course line under the name and through the pages, and no rival's mark does that.Highest. A house with a lapped wall: a stranger names "siding" or "exterior" from the mark alone.13 / 15
C2 Lap AMedium: a crossed-tip A.Low: reads as A, teepee or tent before siding.9 / 15
C3 Reveal TileHighest as a standalone mark: a notched tile.Medium to low: at lockup size it reads as a stack (server rack or drawers) before boards.11 / 15

Pick: C1. It leads on identifiability, and its ownability comes from the system rather than the silhouette. The brief's carrying thread, the course line laying itself, is this mark's own line, so the identity and the site are one drawing.

What it costs. Distinctive scores 3: C1 is the weakest silhouette of the two serious concepts. If Muse rates distinctiveness above identification, C3 is the alternative and is ready. Per S1472, C1 and C3 are not merged.
