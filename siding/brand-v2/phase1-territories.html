Austin Pro Siding: three logo territories

Phase 1, step 1. Each board is one territory: one dominant idea, its type and colour direction, the material it is built from, and what it is not. The marks on these boards are territory prototypes, drawn only to show each direction. After the pick, three concepts are built inside the chosen territory.

The mark: black, white and one accent from the steel family (anchor steel-9 #396791). Type: staple faces only (Montserrat, Inter, Poppins, Roboto). Photos: the firm's own job photos, captioned from its data. References: the annex list only, each with its teardown.

What this territory says

The cut proves the work: you can only draw this edge if you know how the boards hang.

What it deliberately is NOT

  • Not the face of the wall: no stack of horizontal stripes, the logo every siding firm already has.
  • Not a roofline chevron or a house with a chimney.
  • Not a retro badge or borrowed heritage: no script, no "est." seal.
  • Not steel everywhere: steel is the mark and the cut face, nothing else on the page.

Reference card

Scout Motors · https://www.scoutmotors.com/ · job: MOOD

Homepage at 1440 px, observed 2026-11-02 (screenshots in brand-v2/research/scout-*.png).

Why it works
Cinematic full-bleed photography of the product in real terrain carries the feeling, so the interface can stay nearly silent. Heritage is stated with a hard date label ("EST 2022") instead of adjectives.
Principles and small details
The navigation is built as separate white plates joined by small horizontal ticks, as if bolted together. Labels are set in tracked monospaced caps, the voice of a spec sheet. The one accent is a single hand-drawn red underline under two headline words: one drawn mark, used once. The headline is very large and tightly set.
What does not work, not imported
The script wordmark: it borrows heritage and reads poorly at distance. The long paragraph under the headline (a wall of text). The earthy sunset palette. A heavy cookie layer over the first view.
Adaptation
Closely adapted: the hardware logic (parts visibly joined, here the courses overlapping at their butt edges); one drawn accent used once (Scout's red underline becomes the steel cut face); spec-label voice for small labels only.
Original to Austin Pro Siding
Original to this business: the lap-course section itself, a trade-only geometry; the steel family instead of red; no script; the courses as the mark rather than a wordmark-only identity.
Distance check
Scout would not read this as theirs: (1) a geometric mark where theirs is a script wordmark; (2) steel and black where theirs is red on earth tones; (3) a building-trade section drawing where theirs is vehicle photography; (4) wide sans caps where theirs is script plus monospace.

What this territory says

This firm builds in the right order and can show you each layer. The brand is the assembly drawing.

What it deliberately is NOT

  • Not shaded 3D or an isometric render: flat planes only, no gradients.
  • Not a blueprint-blue cliché: the grid is neutral hairline; steel is reserved for the layers.
  • Not a cube or "stack" app logo: four planes in a trade order, one of them cut by courses.
  • Not a diagram for its own sake: labels name layers, never invent numbers.

Reference card

Dropbox Brand · https://brand.dropbox.com/ · job: MECHANISM

Brand guidelines site at 1440 px, observed 2026-11-02 (brand-v2/research/dropbox-*.png). Confirmed CSSDA Website of the Year 2025 winner (official announcement, 12 Feb 2026; top score 9.03, also Best UX Site).

Why it works
The page is its own construction grid: hairline lines run across the whole canvas and every block snaps to them, so order becomes the aesthetic. The glyph is built from flat diamond planes only; nothing is drawn, everything is a plane.
Principles and small details
Chapters are solid colour tiles of different proportions placed on the grid, with empty cells left visible. Large type sits flush to a column. The glyph sits small in a tile corner rather than centred. There is a lot of white between tiles.
What does not work, not imported
Its many-colour palette (this identity is steel only). Its wide display face (staples only here). The open-box glyph itself. The playful colour-pairing system, which would dilute one steel story.
Adaptation
Closely adapted: the visible construction grid as the brand's ground; a mark built from flat planes only; layers presented as tiles snapped to the grid.
Original to Austin Pro Siding
Original to this business: the planes are a real wall assembly in trade order; one plane is cut by course lines; the trim is an L frame; steel depth instead of colour pairs.
Distance check
Dropbox would not read this as theirs: (1) four planes in a stepped exploded stack, not an open box of diamonds; (2) a monochrome steel ramp, not a multi-colour system; (3) a wall assembly subject with labelled layers; (4) Inter instead of their display face.

  • Not a claim in the mark: the dots in the mark are a drawing; only the field behind it is the real record, and it is labelled with the locked wording.
  • Not a halftone texture used as decoration.

Reference card

Cash App brand assets · https://cash.app/press/assets · job: MECHANISM

Press resources and brand assets page at 1440 px, observed 2026-11-02 (brand-v2/research/cashbrand-*.png). The annex names "Cash App Brand Guidelines"; this public brand-assets page was the brand surface I could reach. No separate guidelines URL was found.

Why it works
One colour is owned so completely that a full-bleed field of it is the brand. The mark is a glyph inside a rounded-square container, so it works unchanged as app icon, favicon and avatar.
Principles and small details
The mark and wordmark are carried white on the field with a wide margin and no secondary colour. Black is used only for secondary actions (the "Log in" pill). The container's corner radius is roughly a fifth of its side.
What does not work, not imported
The dollar glyph and the neon green. The glossy 3D product renders further down the page. Rounded pills everywhere. The press page is generic below the hero.
Adaptation
Closely adapted: one brand colour owned as a full-bleed field; the mark lives in a container tile that becomes the favicon and avatar; white on the field, nothing else.
Original to Austin Pro Siding
Original to this business: the field is the firm's real map data; the glyph is a house set in that map's dot unit; steel instead of green; the container is square-cornered like a lot plot.
Distance check
Cash App would not read this as theirs: (1) a dot-set house, not a currency glyph; (2) steel blue-grey, not neon green; (3) a field of real geographic data instead of flat colour; (4) a square-cornered tile and Poppins instead of their rounded container and type.

Graza · https://www.graza.co/ · job: QUALITY BAR (scoped by the annex to custom animation). Observed 2026-11-02 in static frames at 1440 px (brand-v2/research/graza-*.png). The drawn olive carries its own labelled action ("*DRIP*"): the motion belongs to the product and could not be lifted onto anything else. Not imported: the serif, the hand-drawn illustration style, the olive-green palette.

Applied here: each territory's motion is made from its own material (courses hang, planes assemble in wall order, dots fill from the foundation) and each stays under 700 ms, per the motion rules for the logo.

Recommendation, as an input to the pick

T3, The Record, on ownability: no other siding firm can put its own field of photographed jobs behind its mark, and a stranger who sees that field once can name the firm from a screenshot. Its risk is reading as an app: dot-matrix houses exist elsewhere, so the concepts would have to earn the house from the firm's map grid, not from a generic dot font. Its prototype also fills in at 16 px; the concepts need a simplified small-size variant.

T1, The Profile Cut, as the trade-specific alternative: the section cut is siding knowledge made visible, but on this prototype, at 24 and 16 px the wedges and lines start to read as a list icon, which the concepts would have to solve. T2, Wall Order is the most systematic and the most at risk of looking like a generic "layers" icon.
