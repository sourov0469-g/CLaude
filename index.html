05  ·  Workforce Planning

A fixed number,
for every shop

Staffing is not set by supply and demand. Each outlet gets a fixed headcount and keeps it.

WHAT THE COMPANY GAINS
  • Labour cost is known in advance and easy to budget
  • The same team works together and learns each other's pace
  • Workers get fixed hours and steady pay
WHAT IT GIVES UP
  • Idle time is paid for during quiet months
  • No easy way to add hands for a short rush
  • Rush pressure always lands on the same fixed team
OUR VIEW

The rule isn't the problem. The problem is that nothing tests it — a number set on opening day quietly becomes wrong as a shop changes.

06  ·  Recruitment & Selection

Hired in one day

Every extra day of interviewing is another day of slow service — and a good cook takes the first decent offer.

STEP 01
One-day process
Application to appointment inside a single day.
STEP 02
Experience checked
Job-related and fair — though it doesn't prove present skill.
STEP 03
Practical test
The candidate is asked to actually cook. The best idea in the system.
STEP 04
Same-day decision
Fast, which keeps the kitchen running.
THE GAP · NO MARKING SHEET

No scoring sheet or written standard was described. Judge by feeling and two managers pick different people from the same pair of candidates.

The Best Idea In The System

Ask a cook
to cook.

Watching someone do the real job predicts performance far better than an interview or a certificate.

STRENGTH · JOB-RELEVANT GAP · NOT WRITTEN DOWN

A one-page checklist — the tasks, what to look for, a score out of five — would fix it, and it would cost nothing.

07  ·  Turnover

Who leaves?

OFFICE STAFF
Very Low

Fewer similar jobs nearby, deep company knowledge, and a growing chain offers real promotion.

FLOOR & KITCHEN
Medium

Many employers within walking distance. Many are young students who leave for exams or graduation.

THE GAP

"Very low" and "medium" are opinions, not measurements. Without a number, nobody can see which branch is worst — or whether any fix worked.

07  ·  Resignation

60 days, used
as a cushion

MATCHES §27 OF THE LABOUR ACT
DAY 0
Worker gives written notice. The clock starts.
DAY 1–10
Vacancy confirmed. Hiring could start now — often it doesn't.
DAY 10–30
Candidates found, interviewed, practically tested.
DAY 30–60
MISSED: the leaver could train their replacement on this exact kitchen.
DAY 60
MISSED: an exit interview. They've already decided to go — no reason to hide why.

Used properly, the same 60 days delivers a trained replacement and an honest answer about why people leave — for no extra cost.

08  ·  Employee Welfare

What workers get

🏠
Housing

Removes the biggest cost of city living — and makes leaving expensive.

🍽️
Food

A proper meal on a long shift. Costs little in a business that already runs a kitchen.

🩺
Health checkups

Care many low-paid workers would never pay for themselves.

🤱
Maternity care

Fits §§45–50 of the Labour Act, and helps keep women workers.

THE CEILING

Welfare stops people being unhappy. It doesn't make them ambitious. Moving turnover from medium to low needs a visible promotion path, not more benefits.

09  ·  Performance

Checked every month,
not once a year

Industry norm
1× / year
PizzaBurg
12× / year
OPEN QUESTION 01

What exactly is measured? No fixed list was described. Twelve reviews of a general impression is just more frequent guesswork.

OPEN QUESTION 02

Is any of it written down? Without a record you can't compare two months, prove a pattern, or defend a dismissal.

09  ·  Training

A loop that
checks itself

01
Monthly check
Every worker's performance is reviewed.
02
A weakness shows
The review finds the real gap — not a guessed one.
03
Training given
Aimed at exactly what the check found.
04
Next check proves it
The following month tests whether the training worked.
THE CONDITION

This only works if performance is recorded well enough to compare two months. Without records the loop is an intention, not a system.

10  ·  Discipline & Termination

What gets
a worker fired

Breaking rules

Keeps food safety and service the same across 22 shops.

MATCHES §23(4)
Sexual harassment

Credit for naming it at all — many chains don't.

2009 HC ORDER
Dishonesty

Cash and stock pass through many hands in a food shop.

MATCHES §23(4)
Other misconduct

A catch-all — only works if the behaviour really is misconduct.

BROADLY FITS
BUT

Good reasons, applied through a loose procedure. No step-by-step warning system and no legal process were described.

The Cost Of Not Writing It Down

Two gaps. Real risk.
Almost free to fix.

SECTION 24 · DISCIPLINE

A written charge. Seven days to reply. A hearing. A joint inquiry inside 60 days. Skip them and a dismissal can be cancelled on procedure alone — even when the company was completely right.

2009 HIGH COURT ORDER

Every workplace must have a harassment complaint committee. PizzaBurg treats harassment as a firing offence but has none — a rule with nowhere to apply it.

THE MOST URGENT FINDING IN THE WHOLE REPORT — AND ONE OF THE CHEAPEST TO FIX

Everything In One View

Strong points.
Weak points.

Strengths
  • Practical test when hiring
  • One-day hiring process
  • Monthly performance checks
  • Training follows the check
  • Housing, food and health support
  • Notice rule matches §27
Weaknesses
  • No §24 firing procedure
  • No harassment complaint committee
  • Turnover never counted

Opportunities

  • A large young workforce
  • Welfare as a hiring pitch
  • Most fixes are just writing things down
  • Honest feedback from leavers, unused
Threats
  • Labour Court cases over dismissal

11  ·  Major Findings

Told to us.
Concluded by us.

WHAT WE WERE TOLD
  • 22 outlets, a fixed headcount at each
  • Office turnover very low, floor turnover medium
  • Two months notice before resigning
  • One-day hiring, with a practical test
  • Monthly performance checks, linked to training
WHAT WE CONCLUDED
  • Every rule serves one goal: keep the shops open
  • Good at doing, poor at recording
  • The notice rule matches §27 — apparently by accident
  • No §24 procedure and no harassment committee: our two most urgent findings
12  ·  Recommendations

Do these first

Urgent, and nearly free. Not what the company does today — what we recommend it starts doing.

01 · URGENT
Set up the harassment committee
With outside members, as the 2009 High Court order requires.
02 · URGENT
Write the §24 firing procedure
Written charge, seven days to reply, a hearing, a joint inquiry.
03 · HIGH
Keep central worker records
Makes almost every other fix on this list possible.
04 · HIGH
Make a marking sheet for the cooking test
One page. The same hiring standard in all 22 shops.
05 · HIGH
Use a real monthly review form
Turns a frequent chat into usable, provable evidence.

The first two remove the two biggest risks in the whole report — and between them they cost the company almost nothing.

12  ·  Recommendations

Then these

Medium priority — still cheap relative to what they return.

06
Count turnover every month
Leavers ÷ average headcount, per shop. Makes the problem visible.
07
Use the notice period properly
Overlap training, plus an exit interview by head office.
08
Explain welfare, build a promotion ladder
Trainee → kitchen → senior → supervisor, with the pay steps shown.
09
Add induction and supervisor training
Fewer early leavers, and home-grown managers for new shops.
10
Audit against the Labour Act yearly
Confirms compliance — and proves it.

Nearly every item here is a form, a number, or a written rule — not new spending. The method is already sound; this is the paperwork catching up.
