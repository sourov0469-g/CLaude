08b  /  The Cost Of Not Writing It Down

Two gaps. Real risk.
Almost free to fix.

SECTION 24 · DISCIPLINE

No punishment is valid unless the charge is written down, the worker gets 7+ days to reply, gets a hearing, and a joint inquiry with both sides finishes within 60 days. A dismissal done without this can be cancelled on procedure alone — even when the company was completely right.

2009 HIGH COURT ORDER · HARASSMENT

PizzaBurg names harassment as a firing offence — but no complaint committee was described. The rule exists with nowhere to apply it. Neither the complainant nor the accused gets a fair process.

BOTH FIXABLE IN WEEKS, FOR NEAR-ZERO COST — TOP OF OUR RECOMMENDATIONS

09  /  Major Findings

Told to us.
Concluded by us.

WHAT WE WERE TOLD
  • 22 outlets, fixed headcount per shop — a news report confirms the figure.
  • Office staff rarely leave; floor staff leave at a medium rate.
  • Two months notice required before resigning.
  • Kitchen/floor staff hired in one day, with a practical test.
  • Performance checked monthly, linked to training.
  • Dismissal grounds: rules, harassment, dishonesty, misconduct.
WHAT WE CONCLUDED
  • Everything points at keeping the shops open — it's a design, not a pile of habits.
  • Good at doing, poor at recording — sound method, missing paperwork, everywhere.
  • The two-month notice matches §27, apparently without the company realising it.
  • No §24 procedure and no harassment committee — our two most urgent findings.
10  /  Recommendations

Do these first

Not what PizzaBurg does today — what we recommend it starts doing next. Urgent and cheap, in order.

1
Set up the harassment committee

Outside members, as the 2009 order requires.

2
Write the §24 firing procedure

Written charge, 7 days to reply, a hearing.

3
Keep central records for every worker

Makes almost every other fix possible.

4
Build a marking sheet for the practical test

Same hiring standard in all 22 shops.

5
Use a proper monthly review form

Turns a chat into usable evidence.

10b  /  Recommendations

Then these

Medium priority — still cheap relative to the value they return.

6
Count turnover every month

Makes the retention problem visible.

7
Put the notice period to full use

Overlap training + real exit interviews.

8
Explain welfare, build a promotion ladder

Better hiring appeal, lower floor turnover.

9
Add induction + next-job-up training

Fewer early leavers, home-grown managers.

10
Audit against the Labour Act yearly

Confirms and proves compliance.

11  /  Conclusion

Every rule serves one purpose:
keep the kitchens open.

The weak points all look the same: a good method, and missing paperwork. That's what happens when a business grows faster than its office. The job now is to build the paperwork without losing the speed.
