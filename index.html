01  /  Introduction

Why a pizza
chain, not a bank?

In a factory, a manager can catch a bad product before the buyer sees it. In a restaurant that's impossible — the worker and the food reach the customer at the same moment. How you treat staff shows up on the plate.

REASON 01
It's genuinely local

Most textbook examples come from big foreign firms with old, settled systems. PizzaBurg is Bangladeshi, competing against foreign pizza brands in the same city — closer to real life here.

REASON 02
It grew very fast

Around 220 workers in 2020. Close to a thousand today. A company growing this quickly has to build its people systems while running — the strain shows, and that's worth studying.

REASON 03
We could actually get in

The General Manager of Human Resource agreed to meet us face to face. Without that, this would be another report built out of Google searches.

5.2  /  Findings

Hired in one day

Speed isn't laziness here — every extra day of interviewing is another day of slow service, and a good cook takes the first decent offer.

The Best Idea In The System

Ask a cook
to cook.

Watching someone do the real job predicts performance far better than an ordinary interview or a look at their certificates — for a job that means making food properly, fast, under pressure, that gap is enormous.

STRENGTH · JOB-RELEVANT GAP · NO MARKING SHEET

A test only gives the same answer twice if the person judging it marks against a fixed list. Judge by feeling, and two managers will pick differently from the same pair of candidates — a one-page checklist would fix this for free.

THE CEILING

Welfare stops people being unhappy — it doesn't make them ambitious. Moving turnover from medium to low needs a visible promotion path, not more benefits.

OPEN QUESTION 1

What exactly is being measured? Nothing suggested a fixed list exists — twelve reviews of a general impression is guesswork, more often, not more accurate guesswork.

OPEN QUESTION 2

Is any of it written down? Without a record, the company can't compare months, defend a firing, or prove training worked.

5.7  /  Findings

A loop that
checks itself

Monthly check
Weakness found
Training given
Next check confirms

Because the review comes round every month, the check right after training is a natural test of whether it worked. Most companies never get this far — PizzaBurg could, without buying anything new.

THE CONDITION

Only works if performance is recorded well enough to compare two months. Without records, this is an intention, not a system.

5.7  /  Findings

Fixing today.
Not building tomorrow.

Three things never came up in the interview — gaps in our information, not confirmed faults.

NOT DESCRIBED
Induction

The first week is one of the biggest reasons new staff quit early — no onboarding process was mentioned.

NOT DESCRIBED
Supervisor pipeline

A chain that keeps opening shops needs supervisors — training built for the next job up, not just the current one.

NOT DESCRIBED
Training records

Without a record, nobody can tell useful training from wasted training.

The Cost Of Not Writing It Down

Two gaps.
Real risk. Almost free to fix.

SECTION 24 · DISCIPLINE

No punishment is valid unless the charge is written, the worker gets 7+ days to reply, gets a hearing, and a joint inquiry finishes in 60 days. A dismissal done without this can be cancelled on procedure alone — even when the company was right.

2009 HIGH COURT ORDER · HARASSMENT

PizzaBurg names harassment as a firing offence — but no complaint committee was described. The rule exists with nowhere to apply it. Neither the complainant nor the accused gets a fair process.

BOTH FIXABLE IN WEEKS, FOR NEAR-ZERO COST — SEE OUR RECOMMENDATIONS

8  /  Major Findings

Told to us.
Concluded by us.

WHAT WE WERE TOLD
  • 22 outlets, fixed headcount per shop — a news report confirms the same figure.
  • Office staff rarely leave; floor staff leave at a medium rate.
  • Two months notice required before resigning.
  • Kitchen/floor staff hired in one day, with a practical test.
  • Performance checked monthly, linked to training.
  • Dismissal grounds: rules, harassment, dishonesty, serious misconduct.
WHAT WE CONCLUDED
  • Everything points at keeping the shops open — it's a design, not a pile of habits.
  • Good at doing, poor at recording — sound method, missing paperwork, everywhere.
  • The two-month notice matches §27, apparently without the company realising it.
  • No §24 procedure and no harassment committee — our two most urgent findings.
