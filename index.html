03  ·  Workforce Planning

A fixed number
at every outlet

  • PizzaBurg operates 22 outlets across ten towns
  • Each outlet has a fixed number of employees
  • Staffing is not determined through a supply-and-demand policy
  • About 12 outlets are in Dhaka; the rest are spread nationwide
Strength

Consistency and straightforward workforce planning. Labour cost is predictable and the same team works together in each outlet.

Concern

Less flexibility when an outlet's workload changes. A number set at opening is not reviewed as the shop grows busier.

04  ·  Recruitment & Selection

Hired in one day

  • Focus: blue-collar employees — kitchen and floor staff
  • One-day interview process
  • Practical experience is considered
  • Candidates may be asked to demonstrate practical ability
Strength

Better job-fit assessment. Observing the actual task predicts performance more reliably than an interview alone.

Recommendation

Standardise the practical assessment with a written scoring sheet, so all 22 outlets apply the same hiring standard.

08  ·  Discipline & Termination

Grounds for
termination

  • Violation of rules and regulations
  • Sexual harassment
  • Integrity-related violations
  • Other serious misconduct
Strength

Clear misconduct standards, broadly in line with Section 23 of the Labour Act 2006.

Recommendation

Document the disciplinary procedure required by Section 24 — written charge, 7 days to reply, a hearing and an inquiry — and establish the sexual harassment complaint committee required by the 2009 High Court directive.

09  ·  Major Findings

Major findings

  • Fixed staffing across 22 outlets
  • Very low white-collar turnover; medium blue-collar turnover
  • Two-month resignation notice, aligned with §27
  • Practical, experience-based blue-collar selection
  • Monthly performance reviews, linked to training
  • Welfare support and clear misconduct standards
  • Overall: practices are sound, documentation is limited
10  ·  Recommendations

What we recommend

  • Review fixed staffing levels periodically against each outlet's actual workload
  • Standardise practical recruitment assessments with a written scoring sheet
  • Use the two-month notice period strategically — handover training and exit interviews
  • Strengthen monthly reviews with measurable, job-related indicators
  • Integrate performance and training records in one central system
  • Document disciplinary standards in line with Section 24
  • Establish a complaint committee for sexual harassment cases

11  ·  Conclusion

HRM built around
operational continuity

  • Fixed staffing creates consistency across all 22 outlets
  • Notice periods support replacement planning
  • Practical selection supports job fit
  • Monthly reviews support continuous monitoring
  • Further improvement is possible through stronger measurement, documentation and periodic staffing review
